    Methodology & sources

    Data

    Daily vessel transit counts and estimated cargo tonnage by ship type come from IMF PortWatch (UN Global Platform AIS data, modelled by the IMF and University of Oxford), queried from its public ArcGIS API. PortWatch updates weekly with a lag of a few days, so “latest” means the latest settled day.

    Oil and LNG flows per chokepoint: U.S. EIA, World Oil Transit Chokepoints (1H 2025). Hormuz LNG share and bypass context: IEA.

    Signals

    Change vs baseline compares the latest 7-day average with either the same week a year earlier (seasonality-adjusted) or the Jan–Oct 2023 average (before Red Sea attacks began).

    z-score places the current 7-day average within the distribution of the prior year (excluding the last month), flagging moves that are statistically unusual for that route.

    Status: Normal > −10%, Watch −10 to −25%, Disrupted −25 to −50%, Severe < −50%, Surge > +25% (typically rerouted traffic).

    Risk score

    Exposure (0–100) = 75% energy weight (oil share of world seaborne oil ×0.7 + LNG share ×0.3, scaled so ≥25% = max) + 25% cargo weight (baseline tonnage relative to the largest tracked chokepoint).

    Score = Exposure × (0.35 + 0.65 × Disruption), where Disruption averages the drop in total and tanker transits. A big, quiet chokepoint scores moderate; a big, disrupted one scores high.

    Caveats: AIS undercounts “dark” transits during conflict, so collapses can be overstated; scenario outputs are first-order, ignore inventories and demand response, and are not trading advice.

    Data: IMF PortWatch (portwatch.imf.org) · EIA · IEA. Built as an open-source portfolio project. Not affiliated with the IMF.
